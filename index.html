About

About

I am in the final year of a PhD in aerospace engineering at Texas A&M University, working with Elaine Oran on the quenching of gaseous detonations.

A detonation is a combustion wave coupled to a shock: the shock compresses the gas enough to ignite it, and the heat released sustains the shock. Confine one in a sufficiently narrow channel and it will usually quench — though not always, and not always by the mechanism one would expect. My work covers that full sequence: flame acceleration, the transition from deflagration to detonation, propagation, and the eventual quenching or reinitiation.

The applied motivation is detonation arrestor design, the devices that prevent a flame or detonation front from propagating along a gas transport pipeline. The question has become more pressing as hydrogen is blended into infrastructure originally built for methane, since the two mixtures behave quite differently once ignition occurs.

The work is reactive-flow computation on high-performance computing clusters, built on the AMReX and OpenFOAM frameworks. I run established solvers — PeleC, AMRFCT and detonationFoam — and am developing two of my own: PYRO, in AMReX, and CDMFoam, in OpenFOAM.

Resolution is a recurring concern throughout. A reactive simulation only reproduces the correct physics once the grid resolves the relevant chemical and hydrodynamic length scales, and those scales shift from one problem to the next. Establishing what resolution a given result requires is therefore part of producing the result, not a step that follows it.

Before Texas A&M I completed a master’s in propulsion engineering in Trivandrum, on supersonic intakes, and spent a short period at IIT Madras studying thermal resistance at solid–fluid interfaces using molecular dynamics. Away from research I draw, play soccer, collect Japanese manga, and am slowly working through Japanese and Chinese.

Interests

What I’m working on

  • Detonation quenching and reinitiation

    The mechanisms by which a detonation fails in a confined channel: wall heat loss, the three-dimensional structure of the front, and the conditions under which the wave reinitiates rather than quenching.

  • Transition to detonation in fuel blends

    Run-up distances and detonation cell sizes in hydrogen–methane mixtures, motivated by the blending of hydrogen into existing natural gas infrastructure.

  • Shock transmission from open tubes

    The behaviour of the shock once it exits a detonation tube, and its interaction with the exhaust gases trailing behind it.

  • Solver development

    PYRO and CDMFoam, built on AMReX and OpenFOAM respectively, for reactive-flow problems that existing solvers do not cover well.
